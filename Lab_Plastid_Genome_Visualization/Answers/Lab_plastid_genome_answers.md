## Question and Answers

**1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?**  
The plant is *Coffea arabica*, and its accession number is `EF044213.1`.

**2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?**  
The total length is 155,189 base pairs.

**3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.**  
Yes, the Large Single-Copy (LSC) region makes up the top and left side, the Small Single-Copy (SSC) region sits at the bottom, and the two Inverted Repeats (IRa and IRb) are positioned on opposite sides to bridge the LSC and SSC regions.

**4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.**  
Three examples are `rbcL`, `psbA`, and `atpB`.

**5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.**  
An example is `ndhF`.

**6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?**  
An example is `rrn16`, which appears twice because the inverted repeat regions are duplicated.

**7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.**  
`psbA`, which encodes the D1 reaction center protein of Photosystem II, essential for water-splitting and electron transport during photosynthesis[cite: 5].

**8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?**  
The arrows show the 5' to 3' direction of transcription, indicating that genes are transcribed from both DNA strands in varying directions.

**9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.**  
The GC content varies; it is higher and more stable in the IRa and IRb regions compared to the LSC and SSC regions due to the density of rRNA and tRNA genes.

**10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?**  
Relying on a graphical plastid genome map is much more useful than looking at raw DNA sequences or coordinate lists because it instantly turns abstract data into a clear visual story. While raw files hold exact coordinates, they make it hard to see the big-picture architecture. With my graphical map, I can immediately spot the quadripartite regions, see gene clusters, track transcription directions, and evaluate GC content trends at a single glance.
